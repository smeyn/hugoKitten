Hashrate is a late-game metric tied directly to AI Cores and the AI Apocalypse / AI Rebellion mechanic. It measures the raw computational power and development speed of your civilization's artificial intelligence.

### Where Hashrate Comes From

* AI Cores: Each powered AI Core (built under the Space tab on the Moon or ground structures depending on unlocks) generates a baseline hashrate.
* Energy Dependency: AI Cores consume massive amounts of energy (often scaling per core). If your settlement experiences brownouts, hashrate generation stalls or fluctuates.
* Upgrades: Advanced computer science and quantum engineering workshop upgrades (such as Neural Networks or Machine Learning) further multiply your global hashrate.

### What Hashrate Does

Hashrate drives the growth of your total AI Level (G-Level)

1. AI Level Accumulation:
  * As your AI Cores run, hashrate fills an invisible "experience bar" that steadily levels up your AI system over time.
  * Higher hashrate means your AI reaches higher levels faster.
2. Resource Production Bonuses:
  * At lower, controlled levels, AI provides minor passive boosts to science cap, calculation speed, and specific automated operations.
3. The Risk: AI Apocalypse / Rebellion:
  * Once your AI Level exceeds specific safety thresholds (traditionally starting around Level 15), the AI becomes hostile.
  * A rebelling AI begins consuming power uncontrollably, hacking systems, and destroying buildings (such as deleting Moon Bases, Space Stations, or Factories).

### Managing and Suppressing Hashrate

To prevent an AI rebellion while still benefiting from AI technology:

* Antimatter Bases / Containment: Building Antihash infrastructure or using Antimatter helps suppress the growth of runaway AI levels.
* AI Core Toggles: You can turn individual AI Cores off to halt hashrate accumulation and stop the AI Level from climbing further.
* Entanglement & Quantum Computing: Allows for higher AI efficiency without triggering catastrophic unrest.

Hashrate acts as a high-reward, high-risk acceleration throttle: useful for scaling deep-space computing power, but requiring careful monitoring to avoid an unexpected AI uprising.
