In Kittens Game, Engineering is an early-game science technology that acts as a gateway to both physical infrastructure and societal governance.

Acquisition and Costs

  • Science Cost: 1,500 Science.
  • Prerequisite: You must first research Construction.
  • Leads to: Researching Engineering is the mandatory prerequisite for Writing.

Unlocked Features

Researching Engineering provides two primary unlocks:

  • Aqueducts: A building that costs 75 Minerals (with a 1.12 price ratio) and increases catnip production by 3%. This bonus applies to both Catnip Fields and Farmer kittens. Strategically, Aqueducts are often more cost-effective than building additional Catnip Fields once the fields’ prices have escalated.
  • Policies: Engineering unlocks the Policy system, allowing you to officially sponsor systems of government, foreign relations, philosophy, and environmental management.

Strategic Importance

Engineering marks a transition from simple resource gathering to more complex management:

  • Food Stability: Along with Agriculture, Engineering is vital for establishing a food surplus that can withstand “Cold Winters”.
  • Societal Direction: The policy system unlocked here allows for early-game optimization. For example, you can choose the Clear Cutting or Strip Mining policies to boost wood or mineral production by 30% at the cost of some happiness and increased pollution.
  • Path to Culture: Because it is required for Writing, Engineering is an essential step toward unlocking Amphitheatres, which generate the Culture needed for advanced mid-game research like Theology.

Naming Note and Potential Confusion

It is important to distinguish the Engineering technology from other similarly named mechanics:

  • Metaphysics: The “Enlightenment” metaphysics upgrade (which reduces all price ratios by 1%) was formerly named Engineering.
  • Engineer (Job): The Engineer kitten profession is not unlocked by this technology; it is unlocked much later via Mechanization.
  • Artisan (Trait): The Artisan kitten trait was also formerly known as the “Engineer” trait before being renamed to avoid confusion with the automation job.
