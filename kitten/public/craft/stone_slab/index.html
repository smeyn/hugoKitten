A Stone Slab (often simply called a Slab) is a foundational mid-game craftable resource refined from minerals. Each slab is produced in the Workshop at a base cost of 250 Minerals.

Acquisition and Automation

Beyond manual crafting, slabs can be acquired through several channels:

  • Trading with Nagas: The Nagas have a 75% base chance to sell 5 slabs per trade in exchange for ivory.
  • Currency for Zebras: Slabs serve as the primary trade currency for the Zebras, who buy 50 slabs per successful trade to provide iron, metal plates, and titanium.
  • Steamworks Automation: Researching Workshop Automation allows Steamworks to automatically convert minerals into slabs once per year whenever minerals reach 98% of their storage cap. This frequency increases to twice per year with Advanced Automation.

Construction and Crafting Uses

Slabs are a mandatory material for a vast array of essential infrastructure and higher-tier resources:

  • Storage Buildings: Warehouses (2 slabs) and Harbours (50).
  • Scientific Facilities: Observatories (35), Bio Labs (100), and Data Centres (upgraded from Libraries).
  • Industrial and Religious Buildings: Quarries (1000), Temples (25), and Mansions (185).
  • Resource Conversion: Slabs are required to craft Megaliths (50 slabs) and Concrete (2500 slabs).

Strategic Enhancements

Players can optimize slab production through specific Policy/ and technology paths:

  • Masons Policy: Enacting the Naga Relations: Masons policy provides a +2.5% Quarry slab crafting bonus.
  • Titanium Era: Slabs are considered a high priority during the transition to space, as they are the gating resource required to discovery Zebras and begin mass-acquiring titanium.
  • Iron Will Mode: In this specialized mode, the ability to craft slabs is unlocked once you have established 5 Naga Embassies.

Persistence and Resets

As a crafted resource, stone slabs are lost upon resetting the game by default. To carry them over into a new run, a player must possess at least one Chronosphere and the Flux Condensator Workshop upgrade. The amount preserved follows a formula based on the number of active chronospheres and the square root of the total quantity held before the reset.
