In Kittens Game, arbitrage refers to the strategic manipulation of resource conversion and trading cycles to generate a net profit or bypass severe production bottlenecks. Rather than relying on linear accumulation, players use arbitrage to transform high-volume, low-value resources into restricted, high-value capital.

Key Arbitrage Strategies

Strategy Mechanism Goal
Trade Arbitrage Refining Catnip into Wood and immediately trading it to Griffins. Resolving the mid-game Iron bottleneck by bypassing slow smelters.
Iron Will (IW) Loop Trading Iron to Sharks for Catnip, refining it to Wood, and trading that back to Griffins for Iron. Generating a net profit of Iron and Wood in resource-constrained IW runs.
Temporal Arbitrage Shattering Time Crystals to skip less productive years and land on the Redmoon cycle. Converting temporal capital into a $2.5\times$ spike in Unobtainium production.
Positive Resets Building enough Chronospheres to carry over more resources than were spent during the run. Establishing a self-sustaining engine of exponential growth.

Detailed Arbitrage Mechanics

1. Mid-Game Trade Arbitrage

The raw production rates of base resources often become the primary bottleneck for industrial expansion. To solve this, players use the Catnip $\to$ Wood $\to$ Iron loop. This system exploits the high volume of catnip to create wood, which is then traded to the Griffins for Iron. This “trade arbitrage” effectively turns food production into high-value industrial metal, which is much faster than waiting for standard Smelters or Calciners to process ore.

2. Temporal and Cycle Arbitrage

In the late game, the focus shifts to system arbitrage. Because the Redmoon cycle provides a massive multiplier to Unobtainium production, players use Time Crystals as a currency to “buy” more productive time.

  • By skipping the “fallow” years of other cycles, players ensure their Outposts and Space Elevators are always operating at maximum efficiency.
  • When combined with Numeromancy and active Festivals, this temporal arbitrage generates the massive amounts of Unobtainium required for endgame goals.

3. Positive Chrono-Reset Arbitrage

The ultimate goal of endgame optimization is the positive reset. This occurs when your storage capacity for Unobtainium is high enough (at least 8.14 billion) to build 73 or more Chronospheres.

  • At 73 Chronospheres, the amount of resources carried over into the next game exceeds the cost of building those spheres.
  • This transforms expendable resources into compounding capital, meaning every reset starts the player with an exponentially larger foundation than the previous run.
