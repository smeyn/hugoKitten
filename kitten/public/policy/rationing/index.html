Rationing is a mid-game Philosophy policy in Kittens Game designed to mitigate the food-related “cost of happiness” and provide a minor boost to hunting effectiveness. It is particularly useful when your civilisation reaches high happiness levels that would otherwise cause your kittens to consume catnip at an unsustainable rate.

Policy Overview

  • Cost: 3,500 Culture.
  • Prerequisite: Stoicism policy.
  • Mutual Exclusivity: Enacting Rationing permanently locks out the Frugality policy for the remainder of the run.

Core Effects

Effect Description
Catnip Efficiency Reduces the catnip consumption increase caused by high happiness by 10%.
Hunting Bonus Provides a flat +0.1 bonus to all hunting activities.

Strategic Utility

The primary value of Rationing lies in managing the Resource/Consumption Equilibrium as your village matures.

  • Mitigating the Happiness Liability: While high happiness is essential for scaling production, it also causes kittens to eat significantly more catnip. For example, at 420% happiness, kittens consume 320% more catnip than their base rate. Rationing acts as a safety valve, ensuring that a sudden spike in morale does not lead to a catastrophic food shortage or mass starvation during Winter.
  • Hunting Support: The +0.1 hunting bonus is a helpful, albeit minor, addition to your passive or active hunting teams, increasing the yield of Furs and Ivory used for crafting parchment and manuscripts.
  • Choosing Between Branches: Because this policy is locked behind Stoicism, you have already committed to a path of restraint over the Epicureanism branch. Within the Stoicism branch, you must choose between Rationing (food and hunting) or Frugality (which boosts Mint production by 10%).

In some strategic guides, Rationing is viewed as a situational choice; if you have a robust hunting team and sufficient Tradeposts to manage luxury consumption, the benefits of Rationing or Frugality may feel minor compared to high-tier industrial or scientific policies.
