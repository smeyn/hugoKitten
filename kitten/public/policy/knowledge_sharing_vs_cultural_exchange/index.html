In Kittens Game, Knowledge Sharing and Cultural Exchange are mutually exclusive foreign policies unlocked after researching Astronomy and adopting the Diplomacy policy. Because they are in the same policy group, choosing one will permanently lock the other for the remainder of your current run.

Direct Comparison

Policy Cost Primary Effect Strategic Value
Knowledge Sharing 4,000 Culture +5% science income Generally considered suboptimal because science is relatively easy to generate through Scholars and astronomical events.
Cultural Exchange 4,000 Culture +5% culture income Generally preferred because culture is a notorious mid-game bottleneck required for manuscripts and advanced research.

Strategic Considerations

  • The Culture Bottleneck: Throughout the mid-game (the “Theology” era), players often struggle to produce enough culture to craft the manuscripts needed for scientific advancement. Sources explicitly recommend Cultural Exchange over Knowledge Sharing because “culture is slow” while “science is easy”.
  • Science Management: Science income is rarely the primary constraint on progress, as science caps (expanded by Libraries and Academies) are usually more significant hurdles than science production rates. Consequently, a 5% boost to science income often provides negligible benefits.
  • Iron Will Progression: In the specialised Iron Will mode (playing without kittens), where resources are even tighter, some guides suggest the Diplomacy → Cultural Exchange path as a way to bolster early culture production, which is vital for unlocking trade with the Nagas.

In summary, unless you are in a very specific scenario where your science production is lagging significantly behind your ability to store it, Cultural Exchange is the standard choice to help mitigate the game’s cultural bottlenecks.
