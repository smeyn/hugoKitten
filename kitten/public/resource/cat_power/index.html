Based on the sources, Catpower (a special resource generated by the Hunter profession [1, 2]) is reduced primarily through its consumption by game mechanics, including hunting, trade, and certain buildings.

Here are the specific actions and costs that reduce your Catpower:

1. Consumption for Hunting

The primary and initial use of Catpower is for hunting:

  • Each time you initiate a hunt (after researching Archery), it costs 100 Catpower [2, 3].
  • Hunting allows you the chance to gather rare resources like Furs and Ivory [3, 4].

2. Consumption for Trade and Exploration

Catpower is necessary for diplomatic actions and trade:

  • Exploring: Sending explorers to find new races costs 1,000 Catpower [5, 6].
    • If no trading partner is found during the exploration attempt, 950 Catpower is refunded (in the web version of the game) [5, 6].
  • Trading: Diplomacy is a Policy option that, if chosen, makes trades cheaper in terms of Catpower, reducing the cost by 5 Catpower per trade [7]. (The standard cost per trade is not explicitly stated in Catpower units, but the diplomacy policy affects it [7]).

3. Consumption by Buildings

One building consumes Catpower passively while active:

  • Mint: The Mint building, unlocked by researching Architecture [8], consumes Catpower at a rate of $0.75$ per tick [9].

4. Challenge Restriction

In addition to consumption, the maximum available Catpower can be reduced by challenges:

  • Atheism Challenge (Increasing Challenge): For each completion of the Atheism challenge after the first run, the maximum Catpower will decrease by 125 [10].

Summary of Catpower Management

Catpower is a vital early-game resource for obtaining luxury items (furs, ivory, unicorns) [4, 11, 12] and for finding new trade partners [5]. High Catpower limits are necessary for purchasing certain religion upgrades like Templars, which increase Max Catpower by 75 per Temple [13]. Therefore, actions that consume Catpower should be balanced by its production through Hunters (+$0.06$ per tick).
